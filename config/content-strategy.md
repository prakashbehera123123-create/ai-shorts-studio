# Content strategy and topic scoring

Research buckets:
1. Agentic AI and MCP
2. LLM engineering, RAG, evaluation and context engineering
3. New AI tools and meaningful product releases
4. Cloud AI and Azure/AWS AI services
5. Developer workflows and practical experiments
6. AI engineering career/interview concepts
7. Responsible AI, security and reliability
8. How everyday tech works — security, privacy, apps (`how-tech-works`; e.g. how an authenticator code works, why incognito is not private)
9. System design and big-tech engineering (`system-design`; e.g. how a ride app batches matches)
10. Science, space and research breakthroughs with a tech angle (`science-space-tech`; e.g. ML finding planets in telescope data)
11. Other hot technology news of the week (`trending-tech`)

AI topics stay the core of the channel, but buckets 8–11 compete on the same score (owner decision 2026-11-20): pick
whatever is strongest that day. All rules below still apply — primary sources, a mechanism to explain, no hype,
no speculation. Avoid politics, health/medical advice, finance tips and celebrity gossip.

Score each candidate 0–5:
- Freshness (20%)
- Audience relevance (20%)
- Practical usefulness (20%)
- Evidence quality (20%)
- Visual/story potential (10%)
- Distinctiveness (10%)

Reject if:
- No credible source for the main claim
- Topic is merely speculative
- It duplicates a recent channel video
- Rights to required footage are unclear
- Hook requires misleading framing
- No story angle: no tension (paradox, broken belief, black box, before→after) **and** no worked example

Story requirements (from `docs/REFERENCE_VIDEO_ANALYSIS.md`): every selected topic must have one central question, one
worked example and one visual metaphor. Prefer explaining a mechanism ("how/why does X work") over listing features; the
weakest reference was a feature list, and so was our first dry run. "Visual/story potential" is scored on these three fields.

Prefer primary sources: official product announcements, release notes, docs, research papers, standards and project repositories. Secondary sources may add context but must not replace primary evidence for product claims.
